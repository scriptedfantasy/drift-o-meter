FIXED The garage, and the sound · 08:30 754 tests, typecheck clean

Two of the six fix lists are done, and both builders did the thing this project has spent the night learning to ask for: they tried to break their own guard before trusting it.

The garage added a rule to the capture harness saying a certain colour must be absent from a named box — then put the old drawing code back to confirm the check actually fails, at 396 and 264 offending pixels. It also found the test that had been blessing the bug: an assertion that a spun slide’s mark should be 96, which is the instantaneous peak, on an axis captioned “held angle”. It is 91 now, and every mark is asserted to be at or below its own instantaneous peak.

And it killed the class rather than the line. A spun slide gets no height — and neither does any slide of a run the monitor threw out, because that was the same false claim by another route. Both are drawn as footprints in a gutter below the axis. The ambiguous cases are swept deliberately: a spun hold below the ceiling and one above it both draw at zero.

The sound layer’s new test rejected two of its author’s own drafts before passing — a clip’s prose may now only name the tier that clip was measured at, and “the loudest tier is kept for SPIN” is not allowed to sit in a row that is not tier A. It also disproved a number in its own critique (0.75 was a one-track slice of 0.50), and fixed a bug the orchestrator introduced: the grade sound was latched as “already played” before it was played, so on a slow cold start the most cinematic second in the app was silent and could never recover. Traced with a committed instrument showing the clip going from never starting to starting.

Still open, and named by the critic rather than discovered later: the garage is “a very good logbook; it is not yet a podium”. The ridge is honest now and the card is tighter, but a returning driver’s grade still lands with no shockwave, no particle and no gold.

FAIL Replay screen · round 4 · 06:00 6.5 → 7.5 · no severity-one

The defect that survived three rounds is dead. The critic did not take that on trust: it wrote its own 192-run sweep from the contract rather than from the builder’s tests, found zero fabricated scores and zero disagreements with the scorer, then reconstructed the OLD rule on the same grid and reproduced the previous critic’s exact numbers — 50 of 192 runs fabricating, one of them at 24,109 against a session total of 23,982. First round with no severity-one finding.

It also settled two disputes by checking out the previous version of the codebase and re-running the measurements there. The case the last critic cited never reproduced — on either tree. And the rule the builder deleted, reimplemented verbatim and run over 192 runs, disagreed with the new one exactly zero times: so that finding was a real ownership defect but never a wrong number on screen. Deleting it was still right; the framing was loose. Both halves said plainly.

What it failed on is the sharpest thing in this session. The rule written from this screen one commit earlier recurred on this screen. The frame quoted as proof that a not-scored run draws no ember — “24 pixels, so it really is ember-free” — is shot at the one moment the leak is off camera; a different moment of the same run reads 818, a visible orange streak two inches under the words NOT SCORED. And the 24 pixels were never ember at all: sampled directly, they are antialiased gold scrubber pips that happen to satisfy the harness’s own colour test.

That test counts gold as ember, and counts red as ember too — which is why another screen’s 30,591 “ember” pixels turn out to be mostly a red warning banner. A predicate loose enough to swallow both cannot support a claim about either. It is being fixed at the source, along with the thing all of this needs and none of it had: a way to assert that a colour is absent from a region, rather than only that something drew.

FAIL Calibration flow · round 3 · 05:00 6.5 → 7.0

Every number the last round claimed reproduced exactly, and the severity-one it was sent back for is dead everywhere the critic could reach. Then it found the same class in a sixth and seventh place, and one of them is structural.

  1. The gold “mount looks unsteady” banner quotes a sentence that is never about the mount. Not usually — never. The engine’s message answers the root cause and returns its forward-axis sentence before it ever reaches the mount branch, and the banner only appears in the state where that is guaranteed. Measured across 96 runs: 402,569 caution frames out of 402,569. One recording with a perfectly rigid mount reads “MOUNT LOOKS UNSTEADY / Can’t tell which way the car points”. In 2.7 percent of frames the shaking headline quotes a GPS sentence instead — and since the screen has no GPS light, the one place a dropout surfaces is labelled as the cradle.
  2. “As sharp as it gets — it never climbs later” is printed while the number is still climbing. The measurement behind it was correct: final quality is below the peak in 48 of 48 runs. But it compared final to peak, and the sentence is read the moment the screen first says READY, which arrives before the peak. The number climbs after the word “never” in 24 of 48 runs, by as much as 0.209, and the screen’s own verdict flips from “good enough to score” to “sharp” in nine of them — two frames 0.8 seconds apart, same footer.
  3. The four-second warm-up that hides a false reading is shorter than the reading: 30 of 48 rigid mounts still flash “unsteady” past it.
  4. The last threshold this screen still owns puts “Ready to measure” over a light reading “Settling” — in 100 percent of frames at one looseness setting.
  5. Four of its tests cannot fail. One asserts that a sentence exists rather than that it is true, and passes while the sentence is false on screen.
  6. And the disproven 0.74 ceiling is in a third file, citing a document that now says there is no such ceiling.

It also tried a third time to falsify the claim that this screen never saves a run, with 240 more runs across aggression, vibration, tracks, mounts and seeds — the forward axis resolved 240 out of 240, unaided. Its closing line is the sharpest product note anyone has made: the biggest, brightest button on the screen only appears in a state a parked driver can never reach, and it is labelled DONE. On a screen that gates nothing, that is the wrong word.

JUDGING All six pieces at once · 04:00 one frozen build, six fresh critics

Every fix list is done. Nothing has been re-judged since. So one export was built and six independent critics with no shared context are judging the drive display, the replay, the garage, the calibration flow, the verdict screen and the sound against exactly the same code — each told what the builder claims and instructed to verify rather than accept.

The replay’s fourth round closed the defect that survived three: 0 of 192 runs now fabricate a score, where 28 of the first 112 did. It also found two things nobody asked for. The not-scored greying had been applied to thirteen draw sites and was missing four, not two. And the harness route that was supposed to prove that frame rendered had become a check on the bug itself — it asserted a minimum of ember pixels, passed at 240, and the same run was painting its entire lap in full ember, because the frame was shot seventeen seconds in, before the car had slid. At forty-six seconds the count was 39,716.

The protocol now carries both lessons the last two rounds produced. When a screen and your instrument disagree, suspect the instrument. And ask what a pixel threshold would read at a different moment of the same run, because a green check can quietly certify a defect.
