Phase 4 — every screen exists; the last critiques are running
Updated 2026-09-20 22:18 UTC

An iPhone app that judges real drifting from the gyroscope, accelerometer and GPS, and turns a run into a cinematic replay. The whole app is four steps: calibrate only if necessary, press one button, drive, see the verdict. Each piece was built by its own agent and judged by a separate fresh-context critic that re-ran the code and looked at the real output, never the builder's summary.

Latest critic verdicts

FAIL The two screens a driver uses · round 1 · 23:20 HUD 7.5 · Results 7.0

A critic re-shot both screens itself, added 39 moments of its own including the ones a builder would avoid, pulled video frames, and replayed the recordings through the engine to check every number. It found no fabrication anywhere and withdrew its own opening suspicion as false. Both screens fail on narrow, specific things.

  1. Every run opens with a red alarm headed MOUNT SHAKING when the real condition is that GPS has not locked yet. A driver who sees a red alarm each time they press go stops reading the one element that must never be ignored.
  2. The gauge celebrates in gold at 82 degrees on a run the engine is scoring at zero, because the drama never learned about the integrity verdict.
  3. The needle is drawn through the hero number below 25 degrees, which is exactly when a driver is deciding whether the car has taken a set.
  4. In the warning states the STOP control lands under the home indicator, in precisely the state where the driver needs to stop.
  5. On the results page the sloppiest run scores a perfect 100 on the heaviest component while the verdict directly above condemns it. That one is a scoring fault, not a presentation one.
  6. The showcase run repeats every corner to zero point zero metres, because the fixture replays the same lap plan. The page was publishing a simulator artifact as the driver’s achievement.
  7. The grade thresholds, the only thing telling a driver what a grade costs, render at 1.4 to 1 contrast where everything else on the page is above 6 to 1.
  8. Verified good: the angle numeral is 12.5 millimetres at 6.3 to 1 contrast, every number matches the pipeline frame for frame, and the refusal state was called the most honest verdict screen the critic had reviewed.
PASS Replay scene · round 3 · 21:40 9.0 / 10 — PASS

The first piece to clear the bar, after three rounds of being sent back. The critic re-ran its own probes, regenerated every frame from current code and measured before judging. It also withdrew one of its own round-two findings as mis-aimed and disclosed a file it had overwritten by accident.

  1. Camera opening error 0.0000 degrees, against 91.5 degrees of wrong rotation and a quarter-turn whip two rounds ago.
  2. All five grades reachable across the skill range, and the gold top-grade badge renders where it was previously unreachable.
  3. Transitions restored from one per run to six, with magenta measurable in twelve of sixteen frames instead of one of six.
  4. The ghost sits a median 2.7 metres away and is on screen for every in-lap sample, where it used to be absent 88 percent of the time.
  5. Still a top-down map with no perspective. The critic judged that the ceiling on the score rather than a defect, and said the frames would sit beside a stylised racing game without apology.
  6. Remaining, none blocking: the ghost hides under the car at close range, one long drift can ride a single transition, a 70 and a 93 degree slide share a callout, and the opening frame still shows the run’s best angle.
FAIL Replay scene · round 2 · 20:30 8.0 / 10 (bar is 8.5)

The same critic re-ran its own probes, regenerated all twenty frames from current code, and confirmed every severity-one and -two finding as genuinely fixed. It fails the bar on three regressions the rebuild introduced, each one local, and it named the exact line for the first.

  1. The camera now opens every replay rotated about ninety degrees wrong and whips a quarter turn in the first second. A field initialised to zero instead of not-a-number made the heading-seeding branch dead code.
  2. Splitting long drifts collapsed every grade to C on both tracks, so the grade colours that were just wired up can never show anything but neutral.
  3. The splitter cuts long drifts at the deepest interior angle minimum, which in a linked sequence is exactly the transition. It was severing the one moment the design gives its own colour, callout, flash and haptic.
  4. The lap counter now reads LAP 2 of 2 on the opening frame. Previously it read a visible null, which was also wrong but honestly so.
  5. Verified as genuinely fixed: background emptiness 74.3 to 5.1 percent, car 0.151 to 0.511 percent of the window, ghost never the watched lap, zero non-finite camera frames after a bad sample, nothing below 12 point type, cyan down from five jobs to two.
FAIL The judgement layer (detector, scoring, track, integrity) · round 1 · 19:45 3.5 / 10

A critic asked the only question that matters: would a trackside judge agree with this app? It wrote eleven of its own probes and swept 200 full runs across the whole skill range. The detector and the integrity monitor came out strong. The scoring stage between them would have to be rejected outright.

  1. A confirmed spin never reached the results screen. The live display flashed CHAIN LOST while the results screen awarded the same moment a perfect exit, and the saved session recorded no spin at all.
  2. Sawing the wheel back and forth at twelve degrees, which is not drifting, scored 572,345 points against 7,082 for a real forty-degree slide held for thirty seconds. The transition bonus grew quadratically with no cap.
  3. The consistency score threw away exactly the drifts that were inconsistent, because a drift that never settles has no settled section to measure. The sloppiest driver in the grid took the top grade on a 0.62 second window.
  4. Hand-holding the phone paid 40 percent more than a proper mount. The integrity monitor detects this correctly within 0.7 seconds, but nothing in the app was listening to it.
  5. Between 76 and 85 degrees the app silently deleted 82 percent of a drift’s points and told the driver nothing, because the detector and the scorer disagreed about what counts as a spin.
  6. On the main circuit the top grade was unreachable and the whole skill range collapsed into two letters. The same code on the mountain pass spanned the full range, so a grade said more about the track than the driving.
  7. Two of the five component bars were near-constants. One style award fired on 99.6 percent of drifts, another could never fire at all.
FAIL Replay scene · round 1 · 18:40 4.5 / 10

A fresh-context critic wrote its own probes, rendered 26 extra frames including the ones a builder would avoid, and measured the images pixel by pixel. The model underneath is clean and well tested. What it draws is not a replay.

  1. The ghost car was numerically identical to the car being watched for 47 % of the replay, and its gap readout divided a distance by instantaneous speed, so a constant 3.9 second gap flickered between 3.9 and 4.5.
  2. A single NaN speed sample permanently destroyed the camera: 1203 of 1800 frames went non-finite and never recovered, which on the phone is a blank screen for the rest of the session.
  3. Markers from lap one were never retired, so every label on lap two was drawn on top of its own twin, making the same corner look like it had six transitions.
  4. Nothing escalated. The intensity ceiling was set to exactly the simulator peak, so a forced 70° slide rendered pixel for pixel like a 15° one, and the gold reserved for extreme angles appeared in none of the 26 frames.
  5. The world window measured 74 to 91 percent pure background and the car occupied 0.151 percent of it, about 5 mm on a real phone.
  6. Fourteen of 23 text elements were below the iOS minimum size, and the points numeral was the biggest thing on screen instead of the angle.
  7. The telemetry strip showed the final score and grade in the very first frame, so the replay opened by revealing its own ending.
FAIL Sensor simulator · round 1 · 14:00 5.5 / 10

A fresh-context critic re-derived the simulator physics from its own output and failed it. Everything downstream is tuned against this ground truth, so a wrong simulator means a wrong app.

  1. Ground truth teleported 0 → 46 km/h in one 10 ms sample at the start and snapped 33° of heading at the end of every lap.
  2. The hand-held sway fed the gyroscope with the wrong sign, so loose-mount data was physically impossible.
  3. The advertised under-damped initiation flick was dead code; every drift began with a 1255°/s² kink no car can produce.
  4. Linked corners were never actually linked, so the signature transition happened once per lap instead of throughout.
  5. Vibration was three pure tones, which a downstream filter could trivially notch out instead of learning to be robust.
  6. The track did not match its own description: the "right-hand sweeper" was a left, the chicane had no left-right.

What the critics are looking at

How things are verified

  • This build machine is Linux: it cannot compile or run the iOS binary. The engine is pure TypeScript and is exercised by tests against a physics simulator with ground truth.
  • The UI is the real app bundle, built for web, run in Chromium and screenshotted by the harness; critics judge those screenshots and videos, never the builder's summary.
  • What only a phone can verify (real CoreMotion units, haptics, 100 Hz timing, GPS latency in the field) is listed as on-device checks in the final hand-off.
